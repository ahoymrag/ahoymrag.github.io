## How will you use $100,000?

This isn't speculative. I've run the numbers.

**Phase 1 — Summer 2026: Get out and get a space.**
Transition out of the school system. Secure a physical footprint in New Haven—targeting the Audubon Arts District or East Rock. First and last month's rent, basic buildout, liability insurance. This is the part that normally requires a high-interest SBA loan or a co-signer I don't have. The fellowship replaces that.

**Phase 2 — Autumn 2026: Open the studio.**
The full first-year operating budget is approximately $86,000. That covers rent, equipment maintenance, insurance, and enough runway to not make panicked decisions. The Komodo is already paid for. The lenses are already paid for. The money goes into the infrastructure around the tools I already own.

**Phase 3 — Winter 2026: Launch the school side.**
Evening and weekend workshops for New Haven community members—kids, adults, aspiring filmmakers, educators. These generate the recurring revenue that keeps the studio open. The workshop income funds *Creatures in the Tall Grass* production.

**The thesis:** A creative studio can be self-sustaining if it teaches as well as it makes. The fellowship is what gets it to the point where that loop is possible.

## Describe a time you MacGyvered a solution.

I was running a robotics unit in Bridgeport. The district's kit order hadn't arrived—a week out from the lesson. No budget to replace it, no timeline on delivery.

I went to the Dollar Tree and bought a bag of rubber bands, some plastic cups, and a pack of craft sticks. I spent a Sunday redesigning the entire lesson around what I had. The kids built tension-powered launchers instead of wheeled robots. They still learned force, distance, and iteration. They were more engaged than they would have been with the official kit because the constraints made it theirs—they had to figure out why their design wasn't working, not just follow a diagram.

That's the mode I live in. Limited resources aren't an excuse. They're the brief.

## Why now? Why New Haven?

New Haven has an arts infrastructure that most mid-size cities would kill for—the Yale School of Drama, a dense network of working artists, a Night Market that draws thousands—but it doesn't have a scrappy, accessible, production-forward creative space for people who aren't affiliated with the university.

The city needs a place where a 10-year-old from the Hill can pick up a camera and a filmmaker from Fair Haven can cut a trailer. I want to build that place. Not as a nonprofit that's always three grants away from closing, and not as a vanity project. As a real business with a sustainable model.

I've been building toward this for three years with no outside help. The fellowship is the difference between this taking a decade and this taking one year.
