# O'Shaughnessy Fellowship & Grant Application
### Alex Gonzalez — Ahoy Indie Media — New Haven, CT

---

## The Project

I'm building a hybrid cinema studio and community creative space in New Haven, CT. The studio produces original narrative film. The community side runs hands-on workshops—filmmaking, camera operation, storytelling—for kids and adults who don't have access to that world. One funds the other. Neither can exist alone.

The first film out of the studio is *Creatures in the Tall Grass*, a narrative feature currently in preproduction.

The space is called **Ahoy Indie Media**.

## Relentlessly Resourceful
*"MacGyver your way through obstacles with limited resources."*

In 2017, I made a VR film accepted into the Middlebury Film Festival's Beyond the Mountain program—the first festival ever dedicated to VR cinema. The film wasn't a passive experience. Viewers floated down a river. The story around them was dynamically triggered—different objects, events, and narrative beats surfaced based on where they moved and what they encountered. No two viewings were identical.

I built that with the tools I had access to at the time. No studio. No production budget. A story that only worked because I figured out the conditional logic to make it run.

The same year I was teaching VR to community members in New Haven who had never used Unreal Engine. I was writing the curriculum as I went.

A few years later, in Bridgeport, a robotics kit I'd built an entire lesson plan around didn't arrive. No budget to replace it. One week out.

I went to the Dollar Tree. Bought rubber bands, plastic cups, and craft sticks. Spent a Sunday redesigning the lesson around what I had. The kids built tension-powered launchers instead of wheeled robots. They still learned force, distance, and iteration. They were more engaged than they would have been with the official kit—because the constraints made them think instead of follow a diagram.

That's not a one-time story. That's the mode I operate in. Every project I've run has been under-resourced. Every one of them shipped.

## How I'll Use the $100,000

I've run the numbers. Here's where it goes:

**Phase 1 — Summer 2026**
Leave the school system. Secure a physical space in New Haven. Targeting Audubon Arts District or East Rock. First/last month rent, buildout basics, liability insurance. This is the part that normally requires a high-interest loan I don't qualify for. The fellowship replaces that.

**Phase 2 — Autumn 2026**
Open the studio. Year-one operating budget is approximately $86,000—rent, equipment maintenance, insurance, and enough runway to not make desperate decisions. The Komodo and lenses are already paid for. The money goes into the walls around the tools I already own.

**Phase 3 — Winter 2026**
Launch the workshop side. Evening and weekend sessions for the community—kids, adults, aspiring filmmakers. This generates recurring revenue. That revenue funds *Creatures in the Tall Grass* production.

**The model:** the school sustains the studio. The studio validates the school. Each makes the other more credible.

## Why New Haven. Why Now.

New Haven has Yale, a dense arts scene, and a Night Market that draws thousands. What it doesn't have is an accessible, production-forward creative space for people who aren't affiliated with the university. There's nowhere for a 12-year-old from the Hill to pick up a cinema camera. There's nowhere for a first-time filmmaker from Fair Haven to cut a trailer with professional gear.

I've taught in libraries, maker programs, prep schools, public schools, and universities. I know what it looks like when someone gets access to a tool they've never touched before and realizes they can make something real with it. I want to build a permanent space for that to happen in the city I live in.

Not as a nonprofit that lives grant-to-grant. As a real business with a model that works.

The tools are already in my hands. The only variable is time—and the fellowship compresses three years of slow bootstrapping into twelve months of actual building.
